# What never goes in a proposal

A proposal is a commercial document the user signs with their name. Anything in it that is
invented or inflated is a claim the user will have to defend.

## Never

- **Invented social proof.** No testimonial, client logo, case study, review score, award,
  partner badge or certification unless the user provides it and says it is true and
  shareable. No "trusted by" line.
- **Figures nobody measured.** No ROI, revenue, traffic, conversion, ranking or AI-citation
  forecast. No market size, industry average or "X % of searches now go through AI" statistic
  without a source the user gives and that was read on a stated date.
- **Prices from nowhere.** No price, discount or package the user's offer file does not hold.
- **Pressure.** No countdown, "only two slots left", "price valid 48 hours", fake scarcity,
  or a threat about competitors overtaking the prospect. A validity date for the prices is a
  normal business term, nothing more.
- **Fear.** No "penalty", "your site is dying" or "Google will drop you" unless the evidence
  shows exactly that (a manual action message the prospect shared, for instance).
- **Forbidden methods.** No bought links, link networks, mass-produced pages, review gating
  or fake reviews in the scope, even as an option. If the prospect asks for them, name the
  platform rule and offer the legitimate route.
- **Hidden limits.** The proposal says it was prepared from a public crawl, on which date, and
  lists what was not measured (Search Console, analytics, real rankings, AI answers, pages not
  read).
- **Private data.** Nothing taken from the prospect's site beyond what the audit needs. No
  personal details of employees found on the site.

## Always

- The date of the crawl and of every page re-opened while writing.
- Evidence for each finding: URL plus literal string or count, labelled.
- What the client must provide, and what the work depends on.
- What is excluded.
- Effects as ranges that include zero.
- A plain next step the prospect can decline.

## Sending

The skill writes a draft. The user reads it, adapts it to their voice and sends it themselves.
The skill never sends a proposal, an email or a follow-up.

## French deliverables

Proposals are written in French by default: vouvoiement, French typography (space before
« : ; ! ? », « … » quotes, « 1 250 », « 12,5 % »), amounts excluding tax (« 390 € HT »)
with the TTC amount added when the client is a consumer, and dates written out
(« 25 octobre 2026 »). The evidence labels `measured`, `reported`, `estimated` and
`assumed` are shown to clients as « mesuré », « déclaré », « estimé » and « supposé ».

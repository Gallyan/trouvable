# Trust signals

What a reader (and any system that tries to judge a source) can see on the page about who is
behind it. Google describes its own approach in its documentation on creating helpful,
reliable, people-first content and in its search quality rater guidelines: re-read the
current pages at run time, date them, and quote what you rely on. Do not paraphrase them from
memory.

## What to look for, page by page

| Signal | Where it shows | Present when |
|---|---|---|
| Author | byline, author page, `author` meta, Person in JSON-LD | a named person or team, linked to a page saying who they are |
| Why this author | bio, credentials, role, the work they did | stated on the site; never inferred |
| First-hand evidence | own photos, measurements, test conditions, client cases the site may publish | the page shows it; a claim of experience without anything shown is noted as such |
| Dates | published and updated dates, visible and in markup | visible date present; markup agrees with it |
| Sources | links to the origin of each important fact | the fact's creator (vendor, study, official text), not a copy |
| Who stands behind the site | about page, contact page, legal notice, address | reachable from the page |
| Corrections | a way to report an error, a dated change note | optional; a plus for factual pages |

## Rules

- Count only what is visible. Missing signals become questions for the user ("who wrote this
  guide, and what can they show?"), never text the skill adds.
- A credential, award or number of clients appears in the report only as quoted from the site.
- For health, money, legal or safety topics, say that a qualified reviewer named on the page
  matters most, and route factual checks to seo-fact-check.
